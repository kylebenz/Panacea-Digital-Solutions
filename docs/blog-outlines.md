# Blog outlines

The seven article ideas from brand guide §14, outlined so they are ready to draft.
The site has no blog yet; these are planning documents, not published pages.

Ground rules, from the guide's article quality checklist (§14):

- Answer the main question early.
- Use a descriptive title and helpful subheadings.
- Add original examples, templates, or practical checklists.
- Cite authoritative sources for healthcare, legal, or regulatory claims.
- Link naturally to relevant service and pricing pages (anchors from guide §16).
- Add an accurate author/reviewer identity where appropriate.
- Update or remove outdated information.
- Avoid giving individualized medical advice or presenting Panacea as a healthcare provider.

And from the site's guardrails: no outcome promises (reach, leads, patients,
rankings), no invented statistics or case studies, and AI-generated people are
never presented as real clients, patients, or staff. Where a section needs a
fact or a source the business does not yet have, it is marked **[source needed]**.
Do not publish an article with that mark still in it.

## 1. How Healthcare Startups Can Plan a Month of Social Media Content

- **Search intent:** Informational
- **Purpose (guide):** Reach early-stage healthcare businesses and explain content planning.
- **Answer up front:** Start from the three or four questions people ask before they contact you, give each one a video and an infographic, and spread them across four weeks.

**Outline**

1. Start with the questions you already hear (front desk, inquiry emails, sales calls).
2. Pick one goal per piece: introduce a service, answer a common question, explain a process, invite an inquiry.
3. A four-week template: one short video and one infographic per week, each pair on one topic.
4. What to collect before production: service details, approved wording, brand assets, contact details.
5. Review and approval: who signs off on healthcare-related claims, and when.
6. Keep it sustainable: batch planning, a fixed review day, a simple content log.

**Practical asset:** a printable four-week planning table (topic · goal · format · reviewer · publish date).

**Internal links:** "view monthly content packages" → `/#pricing`; "see how a short ad comes together" → `/#demo`.

**Avoid:** posting-frequency claims tied to growth; platform algorithm claims without a source.

## 2. Healthcare Promotional Videos: What Should a Short Ad Include?

- **Search intent:** Informational / commercial
- **Purpose (guide):** Explain structure, message, and CTA.
- **Answer up front:** A hook that names the viewer's situation, one clear message, and a single call to action, with captions throughout.

**Outline**

1. The 15-second structure: hook (0–3s), message (3–12s), call to action (12–15s).
2. Writing the hook: name the situation in plain words; no fear-based framing.
3. One message per video: why trying to cover three services fails.
4. The call to action: one next step (message, call, book) and where it leads.
5. Captions and accessibility: burned-in captions for sound-off viewing; transcripts.
6. Claims and review: what needs client approval before publishing.

**Practical asset:** a fill-in script template matching the site's script demo (hook / message / call to action).

**Internal links:** "AI-assisted healthcare advertisement videos" → `/#ad-videos`; "view sample videos and infographics" → `/#portfolio`.

**Avoid:** watch-time or conversion statistics unless cited **[source needed]**.

## 3. Healthcare Infographics: Ideas for Explaining Services Clearly

- **Search intent:** Informational
- **Purpose (guide):** Show use cases and demonstrate expertise.
- **Answer up front:** Infographics work best for things people need to scan: what's included, steps in a process, what to bring, and answers to common questions.

**Outline**

1. Six reliable formats: service overview, step-by-step process, first-visit checklist, FAQ card, "what to ask before choosing", contact card.
2. The principles: one central idea per image, a clear title, numbered steps, readable type, a contact line.
3. Sizing for where it will be seen (4:5 feed, 1:1, story).
4. Accessibility: text contrast, alt text, and posting the same text in the caption.
5. Review: accuracy of service details and approval of any health-related wording.

**Practical asset:** the six formats as annotated wireframes, labelled as concept examples.

**Internal links:** "healthcare infographic images" → `/#infographics`; "compare content packages" → `/#pricing`.

**Avoid:** clinical instructions or health guidance inside example infographics.

## 4. What to Prepare Before Ordering Healthcare Marketing Content

- **Search intent:** Informational / commercial
- **Purpose (guide):** Help prospects prepare service details and brand assets.
- **Answer up front:** Gather your service details, approved wording, brand assets, and who will review content; that's most of what makes the first month go smoothly.

**Outline**

1. Business basics: services offered, service area, booking or contact method.
2. Audience: who you serve and the questions they ask most.
3. Brand assets: logo files, colours, fonts, existing photos you have rights to use.
4. Wording that needs approval: what can and cannot be said about your services.
5. Privacy: never send patient names, records, or PHI to a content provider.
6. Who reviews and approves, and how revisions work (one consolidated revision per item).

**Practical asset:** a one-page intake checklist.

**Internal links:** "send an inquiry" → `/#contact`; "see package details" → `/#pricing`.

**Avoid:** legal or regulatory statements about advertising rules without citing the regulator **[source needed]**.

## 5. AI-Assisted Video Content for Healthcare Businesses: Benefits and Limitations

- **Search intent:** Informational
- **Purpose (guide):** Set realistic expectations and discuss human review.
- **Answer up front:** AI tools speed up parts of production, but every script, claim, and final file still needs human review and the client's approval.

**Outline**

1. What "AI-assisted" means in practice: which steps use AI tools and which don't.
2. Benefits: faster drafts, consistent formats, affordable volume.
3. Limitations: accuracy of generated visuals and wording; why claims need checking.
4. AI-generated people: never presented as real patients, clients, or staff; when to disclose.
5. The review step: who checks what, and the client's approval before publishing.
6. When AI isn't the right fit (real testimonials, sensitive topics).

**Internal links:** "How do you use AI?" FAQ → `/#faq`; "AI-assisted healthcare advertisement videos" → `/#ad-videos`.

**Avoid:** claims about cost or time savings without numbers the business can stand behind **[source needed]**.

## 6. How to Review Healthcare Promotional Content Before Publishing

- **Search intent:** Informational
- **Purpose (guide):** Emphasize factual accuracy and client approval.
- **Answer up front:** Check every factual statement against your own service details, remove anything that promises an outcome, and have the right person approve it.

**Outline**

1. Accuracy: services, hours, locations, prices, and contact details.
2. Claims: remove promises of results or outcomes; keep descriptions factual.
3. People and images: rights to use them; no patient information; label AI-generated people appropriately.
4. Accessibility: captions, contrast, readable text, alt text.
5. Consistency: same details as your website and booking pages.
6. Approval: who signs off, and keeping a record.

**Practical asset:** a pre-publish review checklist.

**Internal links:** "What does one revision mean?" FAQ → `/#faq`; "send an inquiry" → `/#contact`.

**Avoid:** presenting the checklist as legal or compliance advice; recommend qualified review where regulation applies **[source needed]**.

## 7. How to Choose a Monthly Video and Infographic Package

- **Search intent:** Commercial investigation
- **Purpose (guide):** Help buyers compare volume, scope, and revision terms.
- **Answer up front:** Choose by how many pieces you can actually review and publish each month, then check what a revision covers.

**Outline**

1. Start from capacity: how many items can your team review each month?
2. Volume: Starter (4 videos + 4 infographics), Growth (10 + 10), Scale (30 + 30). Prices come from the site's pricing section and must match it at publication.
3. What's included in every plan: planning, scripting, production, one revision per content item, delivery.
4. Questions to ask any provider: revision scope, delivery schedule, formats, cancellation.
5. When to move up or down a package.

**Internal links:** "view monthly content packages" → `/#pricing`; "send an inquiry" → `/#contact`.

**Avoid:** "most popular" or similar unless verified (O-03); promotional pricing unless the promotion is confirmed and live (O-02). Package terms still pending (V-04 to V-08) must be confirmed before this article is published.
